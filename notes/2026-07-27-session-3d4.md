# 2026-07-27 — Phase 3D-4: event parity for the 3D board

3D-1 shipped the WebGL board with a hole in it, stated in `Board3D.tsx`'s
own header: speech bubbles, the bump flash, fall/respawn animation and the
checkpoint pop were all unrendered. This phase closes it. Every one of the
19 `EngineEvent` variants that has a place on the board now has a visible
consequence in 3D, and nothing the DOM board shows is missing from the 3D
one.

Two of the four were outright regressions against the DOM board:
`ReplayPlayer` has always passed `bubbles` to whichever board the flag
picked, and `Board3D` destructured only four of the five props — so taunts,
which players type into every program, simply did not appear. And
`robot-blocked` drew nothing at all where the DOM board draws a
`.bump-flash` ring. Both are fixed. The other two (falls, checkpoint pops)
are things neither board did.

Todd also took all three optional extras: muzzle-accurate lasers, a
destruction blast, a win flourish.

## What each event gets

| Event | 3D treatment |
|---|---|
| `robot-blocked` | Bump ring on the cell kicked along `dir`; the chassis rocks ~0.11 tiles back off the wall |
| `robot-fell` (pit) | Drops down the shaft the 3D-3 kit models, tumbling, fading past the lip |
| `robot-fell` (edge) | Thrown just over a tile past the rim and falls away |
| `robot-destroyed` | Shockwave ring + seven debris puffs; the chassis goes on the first frame |
| `robot-respawned` | Teleport to the dock at 1.7 tiles, accelerating drop, landing ring |
| `checkpoint-claimed` | Expanding green halo + a flare welded to the claimed ring itself |
| `laser-fired` | Beam from the real muzzle. Board shots stay `--danger`; robot shots take the shooter's seat colour. Impact flash when `e.hit` |
| `damage` | Unchanged — the red emissive pulse already worked |
| `game-won` | Ring burst + 12 puffs over the winner, and a 0.8 s lift on the key light |

Deliberately absent and staying that way: `card-revealed`,
`register-locked`, `life-lost`, `player-eliminated`, `turn-*`,
`register-started`. They are the caption, the register strip and the player
strip, all of which already show them.

## The three decisions that make it work

**Nothing upstream moves — and that means the replay clock never waits.**
`eventDuration()` is shared with the DOM board, so an effect that asked the
cursor to hold would break the drop-in-swap property that makes `Board3D`
cheap. Every effect therefore has a FIXED duration sized under its event's
1× budget (bump 0.34 s in a 450 ms slot, blast 0.55 s in 750 ms, checkpoint
0.50 s in 650 ms, win 0.8 s in 900 ms, fall 0.68–0.72 s in 750 ms, respawn
drop 0.42 s in 550 ms), and any **rig** animation still running when the next
event arrives is force-finished. That is exactly how the DOM board's CSS
animations already behave when the cursor advances underneath them.

**A death animation has to outlive `visible: false`.** The VisualState
reducer hides the robot on `robot-fell`/`robot-destroyed` in the *same*
`update()` that delivers the event, so `RobotRig.setVisible(false)` keeps
drawing while a fall is playing and only honours the hide when it finishes.
Symmetrically, `robot-respawned` sets a new position *and* `visible: true`,
so it teleports to the dock at height and drops in rather than sliding the
corpse across the board.

**Force-finishing on the next event is also what makes scrubbing backwards
safe.** ⏮ and the scrubber re-fold from the start, so a rig can be mid-fall
when the cursor jumps to a state where that robot is alive somewhere else.
Rather than a special backwards rule, `update()` force-finishes every rig
animation whenever a new event arrives and then starts the new one. The
VisualState is always the truth; these animations are presentation only.

## Three bugs the screengrabs caught, that nothing else would have

1. **Every ring effect was invisible.** The shared ring geometry is built
   with `.rotateX(-PI/2)` so it lies in the XZ plane, and `pose()` then
   rotated the *mesh* flat a second time — standing all five ring effects
   (bump, blast, landing, checkpoint halo, win) on their edge. Only the
   billboard puffs showed. Found by looking at the destruction screengrab and
   seeing nothing where a shockwave should be.

2. **Falls started from wherever the ease had got to**, not from the cell
   fallen into. `robot-fell` follows a `robot-moved` whose 0.1 s ease may
   still be running; the trajectory captured `this.pos` (live) instead of
   `this.wanted` (the cell). At 1× it was invisible; stepping fast it threw
   the robot from between two cells.

3. **A corner edge-fall went the wrong way.** The engine emits **no**
   `robot-moved` before an edge fall — the robot never lands out of bounds —
   so the rig's travel is a register stale and `at` alone is ambiguous on a
   corner. Cy, at (0,0) facing W, was thrown north. `edgeFallDir` now takes
   ordered hints and the caller passes facing first, travel second.

## Two design calls worth recording

**Bubbles are culled off-frame, not clamped to the rim.** First version
pinned every off-camera robot's bubble to the nearest edge with a small
margin, which (a) clipped the text and (b) filled the frame with speech from
robots you cannot see — with `follow=action` the camera is often tight enough
that three of four robots are off-frame. `projectToViewport` now culls
anything outside the canvas and clamps only within it, with separate x and y
margins because a bubble is much wider than it is tall and hangs upward from
its anchor. A bubble belongs to a robot you can point at.

**Reduced motion holds a still mark rather than skipping the effect.** For a
transient effect the literal "end state" is *gone*, which loses the
information. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` an effect instead holds a
fixed legible pose (`STILL_T = 0.35`, near the flash peak) for its duration
and then vanishes; falls and respawns skip straight to hidden/placed. The
information survives, the motion doesn't.

**The edge-fall throw is deliberately short** (1.05 tiles). The 3D viewport
is sized to the board itself, so there is no canvas outside the rim to fall
into — the first version's 1.45-tile arc left the frame before it had
finished reading as a fall.

they are `visible = false` when idle, and three.js's `projectObject` skips a

The calibration warning from 3D-3 is real and I hit a second version of it:
the busy loop needs ~8 runs before it settles (231 ms → 23 ms as the JIT
warms), and calibrating against an unwarmed baseline reported a nonsense
0.86× "throttle". Worse, in this harness a CDP session does **not** survive
between tool calls, so the throttle is torn down the moment the call
returns — apply it and measure it inside one call or you measure nothing.

## Verification

- `npm run typecheck` clean. **185 tests green**: the existing 144
  untouched, plus 41 new `effectMath` tests. No engine or visualState test
  changed, which is the signal that nothing crossed a boundary.
- **Event coverage** — the point of the phase. Driven through a temporary
  side-by-side harness (deleted) that put `<Board>` and `<Board3D>` on the
  same props at the same cursor, over event logs produced by the **real
  engine** from crafted states: a Pit Archipelago turn with two pit falls,
  two edge falls (one a corner) and four respawns in 35 events; Grand
  Circuit turns for a wall bump, a board-laser hit, a robot-laser hit, a
  checkpoint claim, a destruction at 10 damage, and a win. Taunts on all of
  them. Screengrabs `screengrab/3d4-*.png`.
- **Scrub-backwards**: stepped ⏮ into and out of a pit fall, an edge fall
  and a respawn drop. Probe now reports `height` as well as the cell, so
  "nothing left airborne" is checkable rather than assumed. Mid-fall the rig
  is below the deck and visible; one step back it is at its cell, height 0,
  visible. Mid-drop it is at 1.25 tiles; one step back at height 0, hidden.
  Back at cursor 0 all four robots are on their starting cells. Zero
  stranded.
- **Reduced motion** verified via `Emulation.setEmulatedMedia`: falls hide
  instantly, respawns place instantly, effects hold a still mark.
  `screengrab/3d4-reduced-motion.png`.
- **Real app, both widths.** 1280: `3d4-replay-1280.png`,
  `3d4-bubbles-1280.png` (three bubbles over their robots, the fourth culled
  because that robot is off-frame). 375: `3d4-pitfall-375.png` — a real pit
  fall on Pit Archipelago, whole board in frame, bubbles readable.
  - The 375 pass found a real bug: a fixed 92 px x-margin squeezed every
    bubble into the middle third of a 327 px canvas. The DOM board scales its
    bubble to `--tile`, so the 3D one now caps at `min(11rem, 34%)` of the
    canvas and the margin tracks it (`min(96, width * 0.19)`). At 327 px the
    margin is 62 px and the widest bubble 111 px — half-width 56 px, so it
    stays fully on screen without being dragged off its robot.
- **Kit-missing path** (`dist/models/tiles.glb` renamed away): exactly one
  warning, zero errors, board builds entirely from primitives, four rigs
  present. `screengrab/3d4-kit-missing.png`.
- **Flag isolation**: lobby, `#/hotseat` setup and `#/editor` without
  `?render=3d` fetch neither `scene-*.js` nor any `.glb`.
- **Zero console errors and warnings** across hot-seat, replay and unmount
  (captured by wrapping `console.error`/`warn` plus `onerror` and
  `unhandledrejection` on this origin only — the shared browser profile has
  noise from earlier phases' dev servers on other ports). Canvas count
  returns to 0 and the `__board3d` handle is cleared on unmount.

## Gate

Todd watches a replay end to end. The risk is **noise**: seven new effect
types firing every few hundred milliseconds can turn a readable turn into a
fireworks display, and the replay is where players actually read what
happened to them.

My own read after one tuning pass (thinner rings, bigger puffs, brighter
peaks): the rings are the load-bearing marks and they read cleanly; the
puffs are deliberately subtle. If it is still too busy the fix is per-effect
— shorten, desaturate, or cut one outright — not a rethink of the dispatch.
Everything is one `EffectField` method per event.

One thing I would flag as the director's business rather than the effect's:
on a small board like Pit Archipelago the camera stays at the whole-board
framing, so a robot thrown off the *south* rim falls out of the bottom of
the canvas (which is sized to the board, with headroom only above it).
That's 3D-5's interest scoring, not this phase.
