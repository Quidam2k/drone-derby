## The decision it turns on

**A reel is a SUBSEQUENCE, not a speed-up.** `visualAt(initial, events, n)`
re-folds from zero for any cursor, so jumping the cursor is already free and
already safe — the property backwards-scrubbing has relied on since Phase 2.
That is what lets a reel play events 34–39 straight after 11–14 with **no
engine change, no event change and no new state model**.

The corollary shaped everything else: `?render=3d` is still off by default, so
the reel had to be worth watching on the **DOM board**. It is — the headline,
the caption and the counter carry it (`screengrab/3d6-dom-reel.png`); the 3D
camera makes it better, not viable.

## Two judgement calls the plan left open

- **The duration cap drops the LOWEST-INTEREST beat, not the chronological
  tail.** The plan's wording admits both readings. Dropping the tail is wrong
  in practice: a turn's climax is usually its last beat (`game-won` scores 1.0)
  and the cap bites routinely at 5 beats × ~3.5 s against a 12 s budget, so
  tail-dropping would cut the win to make room for a laser graze. Tested
  explicitly. One-line change if Todd disagrees.
- **`reel` is `{ beat, until }`, not the planned bare `boolean`.** A boolean
  would leave the scene inferring a beat change from a cursor jump, which is
  ambiguous whenever two beats end up adjacent. `until` (the beat's exclusive
  end) exists because the look-ahead must not reach past the beat — see traps.

## Traps

- **The look-ahead has to be CLIPPED to the beat.** A reel is a subsequence, so
  events past the beat's end are not "about to happen", they are never going to
  happen. Unclipped, the planner frames one of them and drifts off the climax.
  Hence `reel.until` and `lookaheadSpan()`.
- **A reel must never pull back to the whole board mid-beat.** Found in the
  browser, not by reasoning: a beat's lead-out is routinely `life-lost` then
  `turn-ended`, none of it worth a shot, so `planShot` returns null and the live
  rule answers with whole-board framing — a zoom-OUT over the last second of
  the climax, undoing the one thing the reel exists for. The reel branch holds
  its shot when `next` is null; the next cut is the next beat.
- **`cuts()` cannot answer "does it cut only on a beat boundary?"** It counts
  eased re-aims and hard cuts alike. `hardCuts()` was added for exactly this,
  because inferring the answer from a re-aim count is the eyeballing 3D-5
  replaced. It is 0 for a whole normal replay.
- **The reel REPLACES the replay screen rather than layering over it.** Two
  mounted `BoardView`s under `?render=3d` would be two WebGL contexts, two tile
  kits, and a `window.__board3d` pointing at whichever won the race.
  `ReplayPlayer` stays mounted, so cursor/speed/mute survive ✕.
- **`LEAD_IN + LEAD_OUT == MERGE_GAP` is not a coincidence** — it is exactly
  the point at which two runs far enough apart not to merge are also far enough
  apart for their padding not to collide. `pickBeats` still splits collisions at
  the midpoint between peaks, because `opts` can break the identity.
- 3D-5's browser traps all still apply: **unregister the SW and clear caches
  after every rebuild**, and **`page.bringToFront()` first** or rAF throttles to
  ~1 Hz and every timing number is nonsense.
